BMF (Brand-Market Fit)
I don't care if you're an AI startup founder, a micro creator looking to grow your brand, or an F500 company. You need to get this right.
Everyone knows about product-market fit. But very few think about brand-market fit.
Brand PMF is when a specific audience instantly understands what you stand for, deeply desires more of it, and can’t easily get it anywhere else.
This is the "DNA" of your brand. 
The reality is, most brands position themselves next to 50 other competitors and wonder why they struggle to stand out.
 
Great positioning 101 
Own a category (or better yet, create one)
The strongest brands aren’t just the best in a category; they’re often the only one in it.
Examples
@fomo - I can't even think of another finance x social trading platform.
@Polymarket & @Kalshi - Again, I don't even know of any other prediction market platforms.
@duolingo - While the company has recently declined due to AI, for a long time, they were the only ones that gamified the process of learning.
I can think of dozens more, but ultimately, you have to think deeply about what makes your product different, and lean into it with your branding. If you're struggling to find the answer, it's a sign you need to revisit your product-market fit. This is why a strong product matters tremendously if you want to build a magnetic brand.
Great category positioning is often: 
Combining two worlds (often hot niches): AI x trading, social x trading, fitness x finance, gaming x education, etc.
First mover advantage. Having a great idea and executing first. Even if competitors emerge in your category, if you build the brand correctly, you'll still be known as the #1. 
Solving a specific pain point. Again, branding and product tie directly into each other. Think deeply about the specific pain point your product solves, then start building a brand around it (if you're a creator, think about the pain point / value you're adding with your brand)
2.     Decide the emotion
What do people FEEL and THINK about when they see [your] brand?
Do they feel cool? Do they feel a higher status? Do they respect/relate to you because you have something they want? 
 
3.     Tell the story publicly 
This piece is very important for personal brands
Audiences don’t connect with brands that only ever win. It feels robotic and scripted. 
I’m not saying you have to post your lowest moments, but you do need to show the real journey behind the brand.
Share:
Your hot / honest takes 
Raw videos with your face (faceless brands are harder to build)
What you're struggling with 
What you're building 
And so on. 
4.     Make the audience the hero 
aka: what value do you give your audience? When they interact with your brand/product, what are they tangibly getting in return for spending their attention with you?
5.     Say it the same way, everywhere (messaging) 
The format of your messaging will change across distribution channels, but the story, the product's usefulness, and the emotion shouldn’t. 
TL;DR: BMF is a combination of your brand's values, great product, messaging, and your ICP's psychology. 
 
 
Founder-led versus Brand-led Content 
Exploring the various options for how to actually publish your brand
Founder-led
One of the most powerful brand-growth strategies is founder-led content, with the founder serving as the face of the brand. Often in first-person tonality - sharing opinions, stories, and personality. 
Content angles for founder brands:
How I'm building [x]
Behind the scenes of [x] 
Lessons of a founder 
etc. 
 
Brand-led 
A more "corporate" approach to content. Often speaking in third-person tonality. 
Content angles for brand accounts:
Product launches / updates 
Campaigns 
Community interactions
 
For companies, in 2026, you should be building both. Think of it as:
Founder account: why we’re building this, what I believe, what I’m learning, personal brand 
Brand account: product launches, updates, general distribution 
Note: For personal brands, you are the face. If you're serious about building a brand, you should dox. Your creative options open up tremendously (video content, photos, etc.), and by doxxing, you instantly kill ~80% of the competition that won't show face. Your trust, authority, and relatability instantly go up. It's one of the easiest, highest-leverage things you can do.
(not to mention you'll make more money from sponsors, get access to better private rooms, etc.)
 
How to Grow the Brand 
Ok. We've covered: brand positioning, BMF, and the content angles you'll use (founder and brand), but how do you actually grow?
Funnel construction 
You'll use three primary weapons to grow the brand through a funnel process. 
 
Generally speaking
TOF: Top of Funnel
Goal: reach people / impressions. 
This is your growth content. It’s broad and shareable.
Trendy content 
Outlier virality formats 
Short-form / clipping 
2.     MOF: Middle of Funnel
Goal: turn new followers into people who trust you.
This is where you build more authority with deeper-dive content. Sometimes your MOF content will still go viral.
How tos
Long-form
Step-by-step tutorials 
Case studies and breakdowns 
Behind-the-scenes 
3.     Bottom of funnel 
Goal: turn trust → customers (if you have a product) 
This is where content often asks for a specific CTA.
Content that works:
Product demos and walkthroughs
Customer results and testimonials
Offers, launches, and limited-time deals
Free resources that lead into paid ones (lead magnets)
FAQs and real-time objection handling
Depending on your brand, you can skew your resources towards different parts of the funnel. 
For example, if you're building a finance news 𝕏 account, 90%+ of your content should probably be TOF. 
If you're building a product page on Instagram, 90%+ of your content should probably be BOF. 
You'll have to work out the exact percentages for each pillar you want to allocate to. 
Establishing content pillars 
Your content pillars fit inside the three parts of the funnel. They are generally the 3 to 5 topics/formats your brand is known for. 
 
How to choose your pillars
Good pillars sit at the overlap of three things:
What your audience cares about: their problems, goals, and interests
What you’re credible on: your expertise, experience, and results
What connects to your product: topics that naturally lead to what you sell
 
Every platform has its own audience, format, and culture. Your job is to package and match the content pillars to each respective platform.
 
Renting vs owning your audience
Social platforms rent you an audience. Algorithms change, accounts get restricted, and you can lose your distribution overnight.
Owned channels (newsletter, community, email list) are generally yours forever, as long as you don't violate TOS or get severely banned. 
The goal of every distribution strategy: use rented platforms to find people, and owned channels to keep them engaged with the brand. 
 
Building a Cult Community 
There's a massive difference between building an audience and building a community. The best brands in the world have a genuine community to back them.
Make people feel special for being a part of your inner circle
A name
Public recognition 
Visual identity 
Shared beliefs / language 
A qualification process to allow access 
2.     Give them a home to interact 
 
3.     Stay close to the roots of the community 
If you're a founder, spend time interacting in your private groups 
Answer questions 
Go live-stream for the community 
Share behind-the-scenes info
And so on. 
4.     Create rituals 
Weekly events 
IRL retreats / annual events / meetups 
Member spotlights 
Challenges 
Good example: think about how tech companies like OpenAI and Google have dedicated "Dev Days." 
 
Final Tips & Some Advice 
Don't neglect TOF content. This is what allows any brand to grow consistently over time.
Pick a couple of platforms to master. If you can master X/YT, then all other channels become 10x easier
Stay consistent. Do whatever you need to stay in the game. 
Set up a content cadence. Establish when you write, film, batch content, etc. 
Most pieces of organic content should have a job. New eyeballs, build trust/authority, or conversion. 
Start your newsletter on day one. Even if you can't commit to publishing regularly, build it up anyway. 
Repackage and study your winners.
