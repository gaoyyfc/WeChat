1. TikTok UGC explained
The fewer followers they have, the better.
Forget everything you know about social media.
"UGC" is simply user generated content created by everyday people, instead of by companies. Most videos you see on TikTok qualify as UGC. They are unbranded, natural and spontaneous. Think of some girls doing a TikTok dance, or a random cooking video by a hobbyist showing their finished food.
Over time, companies started to catch on. People skipped through their traditional advertisements instantly, but trust UGC content enough to doomscroll and watch this type of content for hours. 

Consequently, companies started trying to mimic the "unbranded feel" so people start watching their advertisements for longer. After all, attention = money.  The goal is answering this question:
How can we make our branded content look like unbranded content?
This is what the whole UGC industry is about. 
Sell to your users, without making them feel like you're selling something.
Make your advertisement feel like a random video containing a recommendation by a friend. This is the key to going viral.
Good UGC Example
Make your videos look unprofessionally professional.
https://www.tiktok.com/@biana.beauty7/video/7691414015246863638
Watch the link above first. 
Dissecting one of my successful UGC video examples:
Snapchat caption - Snapchat is an app where GenZ takes spontaneous videos and shares them with their friends. You can recognize them by the black horizontal text bar. Because Snapchat videos are known for being raw and unfiltered, this helps our goal in making the video look natural.
Slang inside caption - Caption does not use professional language. "broo theres a new tiktok update" is typical GenZ slang.
Creator does not look directly in the camera - Looking in the camera feels scripted. Looking away from the camera feels spontaneous and natural.
Camera angle - Only part of their face is visible so it looks like they just started recording the video randomly.
Recording a secondary phone - Instead of inserting a raw screen recording, the creator films a second phone so it looks more in-the-moment. 
Natural facial expression - Use a natural facial expression like irritation, subtle surprise or sadness. DO NOT USE A SHOCK EXPRESSION. Do not make the facial expression too strong or it will feel fake and scripted.
Natural hook - Rolling the eyes was a good hook. You can also experiment with eating a chip, or running inside a room. Anything that involves some movement or motion works well.
Low follower count - People trust accounts that are unbranded. An influencer recommending a product is an advertisement. A random person recommending a product is much more trustworthy.
The best way to judge a video is to have empathy. 
Imagine that you are your ICP.
Watch the video as if you've never seen it before.
Then judge, if the video feels scripted or not.
Then keep iterating until the video does not feel scripted.

Best UGC Formats
All formats can be split in these 3 categories. 
I rank them in this order:
1. Hook + demo
2. Talking videos
3. Long text videos
Hook + demo
https://www.tiktok.com/@s.ummerspretty/video/7681851466222439710
My preferred option. A mostly visual, short video of about 15 seconds. It consists of a visual hook + payload: a short demo of your app. 

The demo is engineered to go viral, you can't just put a random screen in this demo part. It has to grab attention. More about this in chapter 2 - Viral Feature Engineering.
Talking videos
https://www.tiktok.com/@kiley.zen11/video/7634280277711342862
Contrary to most hook+demo, these videos contain speech. 
This usually works better for more complicated app niches, for my niche this did not work very well in converting users. Also, it's harder to learn for creators since not every creator is well-spoken.
You can combine Talking videos with a demo, but it's hard to do so naturally. Definitely applicable for some other app niches like religion or languages, niches that do not have a visual result like beauty.
Long text videos
https://www.tiktok.com/@kiley.zen11/video/7642467371646536973
Mostly used for brand-awareness or warming up the account. This video does not convert many users to download your app, and the users that do download your app download it out of curiousity rather than buying intent.
The advantage of Long text videos is that it's very simple to make, and easily hits a good amount of views. 
For my UGC campaign, I almost exclusively used hook+demo videos. As you will see in my videos, they are pretty much all the same format.
How to find your own format
The best advice I can give you is to look at what other apps are doing and note the similarities. 

For me for instance, I noticed that the Share Extension feature from Apple (sharing a video inside Tiktok to your app) was a very common format for many apps. So my viral video formats are based on this. I created the "Stealing your makeup" feature; Share your TikTok video to my app and it will generate a look and list make-up products to use to copy the look in the video. 
Look at what others are doing, then be creative and think of something that can go viral in your niche.
You have to have a feel of virality. I just "knew" that this is something that would go viral. Women like to watch celebrities and want to know how to get the same look. 

Also "Stealing" sounds illegal, and illegal tends to go viral. Obviously this is an exaggeration and my app does not really "Steal" anything. It's just metaphorically copying the make-up look celebrities have. 
To get your spider-senses ready to know when something is potentially viral, start doomscrolling for 2 hours in your niche and become familiar with your ICP. Start doomscrolling other apps and keep your eyes open and wide. It's just pattern recognition.
The magic happens in the details. One detail is not noticeable, but 100 details combined make a successful and viral video.
 
2. Viral Feature Engineering
It's one thing to be able to create a natural-looking video.
It's another thing to create a natural-looking video while selling something, while going viral.
Viral Feature Engineering (VFE) is the art of adding a feature to your app that's purely designed to go viral. 
Think of it this way; 
-You have 15 seconds in a TikTok video. 
-You have around 5 seconds to sell your app. 
-You cannot explain your whole app in 5 seconds. 
So you have to engineer something in your app that's attention-grabbing. 
My viral feature: Steal their make-up
https://www.tiktok.com/@s.ummerspretty/video/7682600670096067871

For my beauty app, I created the "Steal their make-up routine" feature. 
It works by sharing a TikTok video to our app. Then, my app "steals" the make-up look in that video and shows products to achieve this look.
 
This viral feature is what all my UGC content is revolved around. 
Your job is to create a viral feature specifically designed to be shown on social media. The best way to get ideas is to doomscroll alot on TikTok and see what other apps are doing. Then make a spin-off for your app.
You now understand the complete formula of creating good UGC content that sells. 
 
3. Hiring Canvas UGC Creators
Do not hire influencers. Their audience saturates.
Do not hire regular UGC creators. They are overpriced.
Hire Canvas UGC creators only.
This article is written by an indie, for an indie. We do not have millions to spend. We need to find the best way to distribute the good UGC content we just talked about.
Canvas UGC
Canvas UGC is nothing more than UGC but done from newly created accounts (a new account = "blank canvas"). They then spam the same format over and over again on that account by posting 1-3x a day. 

Why do we create brand-new accounts and not re-use existing accounts?
Trust. People trust accounts more with low followers more than influencers with millions of followers. Having low followers is better.
Niche. For every campaign, you need new accounts. You cannot create beauty content and sports content on the same account, it lowers video performance.
Accessibility. Hiring becomes easier since every creator starts from scratch, so you can technically hire anyone even your typical 21 year-old college student.
Budget. Piggy-backing on point 3, content becomes very cheap. Instead of paying $100 for 1 video in regular UGC, you pay $100 for 50 videos.
FYP.  Nobody watches videos of influencers specifically, everybody watches videos on the FYP.  We capitalize on the fact that modern-day social media algorithms put almost no emphasis on follower count for virality. 
Outreach methods
The TikTok DM limit per account is normally 30 DMs a day.
The best way to find Canvas UGC creators, is via 
Reddit - Browse on subreddits like r/canvasugc
TikTok/Instagram - DM canvas ugc creators directly on there. 
Reddit is easier since you get warm leads. You create a post and people DM you so they are the ones reaching out first. This gives you leverage.

TikTok/Instagram is harder, since you need to do cold outreach - You are the one reaching out first, they have leverage. 

But I got banned from Reddit by posting too many job offers, so I had to do method 2.

For cold DM outreach, the best way is to DM creators already working for other apps. This is because they are already used to getting performance-based payments and are also more skillful in perfecting viral formats. Here's my outreach script:

 
Your goal is to secure an invite to your Discord server, not to close the complete deal in DMs.
Look for:
Creators that fit your niche. For me that was women, usually younger / GenZ.
Creators that already do Canvas UGC for other apps
Bonus if they went viral before
Outreach account
Nobody is going to answer DMs from an anonymous, 3 follower account.
For direct DM outreach, use an account whose profile looks trustworthy. 
Make sure your account looks good, represents your brand and MOST IMPORTANTLY looks popular. Make sure your account has atleast a couple thousand followers.
You will get a response in 1 out of 100 DMs. This is normal.
There is no penalty for DMing people too much on TikTok. You will get blocked for the rest of the day if you hit the limit, but it won't ban your account or anything. Just DM 30 people a day.
Virtual Assistant
You can hire a Virtual Assistant from Upwork or Fiverr or any freelance platform to do this outreach for you. This is much easier, but costs extra money. I paid my virtual assistant from the Philippines $100/month, for 4 hours a week. Which is often a decent part-time salary for people from South East Asia. Just give your VA the script and tell them to do outreach from Monday - Friday. It's worth the money, and you can recruit them later for more important jobs.
Close the deal inside Discord
When they agree to the terms in DMs, send them a link to your Discord server. This is the place where you send them the contract and close the deal. See more in the next 2 chapters.
 
4. Payment structure
A good payment structure allows you to scale without much upfront cost. If you have a proven app, you can start with 3 creators and a budget of $1000.
I finally understood how these successful indie app builders were able to have large creator teams without millions in funding. It was a combination of employing Canvas UGC creators, with a good payout structure.
In short, the best payout structure consists of a small base retainer per video + performance bonus.
Specifically, I use $10 per video + a laddering CPM bonus and a payout cap of $3000 to save your profit margins in the case of your creator going mega-viral. They then post 2x a day, across multiple platforms, usually the same format over and over again.

Here's my most up-to-date creator contract:

 
Why would you pay for bad content? 
Add a minimum view requirement for the base retainer.
Key takeaways:
Add a minimum view requirement of 1000 views. This is key. 
The baseline of a normal video on Tiktok is about 300 views. So unless the creator makes good videos, they only earn the CPM bonus and not the $10 base per video. In practice, this will save you thousands of wasted $$$.
The contract should be set to be terminated at-will. So if you see that things are going badly, you can just quit the contract immediately. This makes UGC low risk.
Add a payout cap. Your profits will thank me later when you go viral. 
Let them post 1-3x a day depending on what you need. 
Start with 1-2 formats for your campaign. I just used 1 format.
Cross-post every video on multiple social media platforms. I did TT/IG/FB.
Treat your creators with care. 
They put a lot of trust in you by working for you.
Never do up-front payments, payments are monthly.

Most creators, even millennial creators, are already familiar with Discord.
You need a place to manage your creator team, accept candidates/warm-leads, and make announcements. So I heavily recommend Discord.

Don't just copy me. Analyze what I'm doing and improve on what you see.

Accounts

6. UGC Creator workflow
Create the best environment in which your creators can thrive. 
You give them all the tools and they execute.
You have to properly onboard your creators and create the best environment in which they can thrive. Be concise in giving directions. Be clear with deadlines. 

When the creator is just new, do the following:
Create a timeline of what is expected from them.
 
2. Explain the submission schedule. Explain:
When should they submit their videos for feedback before posting? 
 
3. Answer any questions they have. 
Submission schedule
Before your creators are allowed to post the videos they create, they first have to send the videos to you for feedback. 

This way, you can improve the quality and help your creators improve in skill. 

Create a submission schedule like: 4x video on Monday, 5x on Wednesday and 5x on Friday. I used this schedule for my 2x videos per day posting. 
 
The submission schedule makes up the larger bulk of your work as a manager. Eventually when the creator gets good at replicating the format, you can drop the submission schedule.
Monitoring
Virality is luck, but hitting breakout videos is not. 
That's why breakout videos are the best proxy to aim for.
Your creators are now posting daily and you give feedback frequently. Monitor the performance of the creators weekly and see if their views grow. 
Try to aim for breakout videos. Breakout videos are videos with 10,000 views. They display exceptional performance on TikTok, and hint at potential virality if you keep posting the same format.
When you have a breakout video, take a look at what the creator did right and reproduce this behaviour. 
Firing creators
Since the payout structure is really favorable, I rarely fire creators. This is because if they underperform, I do not pay them out a lot of money.
However, I still fire underperforming creators because they do take time and energy that you could use on other endeavors.
Good creator timeline milestones
A good creator often follows this timeline.
First week: first video with 1000 views
Second week: first breakout video of 10,000 views.
Third week: first viral video of 100,000+ views.
Reasons to let go of creators
Consistently bad attitude
Misses deadlines repeatedly
No breakout videos after a long while
 
My mistakes
Learn from my mistakes. They are listed in order of severity.

No viral feature
You have to have a viral feature to display within 5 seconds. My original UGC videos did not have this, so I got millions of views with little downloads. UGC + no viral feature = recipe for disaster. Never do UGC without VFE.
No payout cap 
My original contract did not include a payout cap, because I thought a CPM of $0.3 would never be unprofitable. Yet, because I went mega-viral my profits could have still been bigger if I added a payout cap in my creator contracts. I now always add a cap of $3000.
Buyers intent with UGC is inherently lower than with ads.
My download-to-paid rate was only 0.5% for UGC, and 7% for ads. I know hard paywall apps are super popular, but people coming from UGC are much lower intent so a free trial or freemium model might work better for you. When you start getting loads of traffic, try A/B testing pricing model. I have a theory where UGC works best for apps that already have a high k-factor.
Hire slow, fire fast 
I hired many creators too quickly so I ended up with some bad apples. If you just start out, max 5 creators is a good starting point. Don't just hire anybody, control the quality that comes into your team. This also applies from a psychological perspective: Hire optimistic creators and work becomes fun. Hire negative creators and work becomes a chore.
 
UGC lifespan
Eventually, purchase intent starts to drop. Millions of users have already seen my app so my videos start having creative fatigue and only low quality traffic comes by.

Do not think that 1 campaign lasts forever, keep iterating on new formats and remember that there will be a day where you have to wrap things up especially if you run hard-paywall. Freemium apps with high retention last much longer.
