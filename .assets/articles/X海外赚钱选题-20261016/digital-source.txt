2) Create your Dream Customer
To sell your product, you need to know who you’ll sell it to.

3) Write your promise
Specificity sells.
I see far too many creators try to hide behind vague promises because they’re too scared to deliver on their promise.
But the truth is, if your promise is vague, fewer people will buy it.
At first, you’d think it’s counterintuitive, but it’s not.
Think about this for a second.
Let’s say you’re trying to plan a trip and you have 2 options.
Option 1) A $1,000 mystery trip.
It could be the best trip of your life, or it could be the worst trip where you go to an underdeveloped country, a 1-star hotel, with the worst airlines possible, the food is horrible, and there are no hot showers.
Every bad outcome you can think of is there.
There is nothing good about it.
Or...
Option 2) a $10,000 trip.
You’re going to your favorite country. The best airlines possible. A 5-star hotel. The best restaurants. Transportation is handled.
You don’t need to think while you’re on that trip because everything has been taken care of for you before you even step out of the door and you know exactly what’s happening at every hour.
Which ticket do you think will sell more?
The second one.
The reason is simple. It’s specific and it reduces uncertainty.
That’s what your promise should do.
It gives people certainty of what they’ll buy.
Don’t write “Grow an audience”. It’s too vague.
A better promise is something like this: “Grow by 1,000 followers per month using the MAGNET framework”.
I’m awful when it comes to coming up with names for frameworks. But you get the idea.
Here’s a formula you can use:
The Promise Formula:

“Helping [WHO] go from [HELL] to [HEAVEN] so they can [BENEFIT(s)]”

4) Create your curriculum
Your curriculum is the steps your customers are going to take to achieve the transformation.
The problem with courses is that they’re static.
If the person who bought gets stuck on a concept, or tries to apply what is being taught, they can’t get feedback.
It’s not like you can ask the creator and they’ll respond.
But with Eden, you can add your knowledge to it and have an AI version of yourself teaching people the course.
This does 2 things:
It increases the perceived value of your products. Because now you have an AI component to it.
It helps your buyers to apply what is being taught properly. So they get better results. Which means better testimonials for you. Which means you can get more buyers (because your product works).
So how do you create your curriculum?
Go back to your promise and ask yourself:
What are the 3-5 major milestones your customer needs to reach to achieve the transformation? These will be your course modules.
For each major milestone, what are the 3-5 action steps they need to take to achieve it? These will be your module lessons.

2) The Problem
Traditional copywriters call this section “The Lead”.
I won’t act like I’m a copywriter, so I’ll just take their word for it.
But a problem I see most people make when it comes to this section is they state the problem, then they write “Keep reading if you’re...” then throw a ton of bullet points hoping that something will stick.
Don’t do this.
It just screams that you don’t know who your Customer Avatar is and you’re hoping that someone buys your product.
Be specific. Call out your Customer Avatar’s problem and make them a promise.
That’s all what you need to do.
Don’t overcomplicate it.
3) The Process
Here, your goal is to show people why your way of doing things is different from everyone else and why your way of doing things is better.
You can lead with your story and show people how you came to your solution.
Let’s say you’re selling a LUT pack for the average Instagram user who just wants to upload high-quality images to their feed.
You can write about how you wanted to upload high-quality images to your Instagram feed but every course or tutorial you watched just made the process more complicated.
Then you learned how to use editing apps (like Photoshop and Adobe Lightroom), but you hated how you always had to start from scratch.
So you created your LUT pack presets to help you quickly edit photos and make them look cinematic without going through the hassle of learning editing apps and using nothing but your iPhone.
If you can add an illustration to breakdown the process is even better.
4) Features + Benefits
This is very straightforward.
You will tell people what’s inside your product.
The easiest way to do this is to write bullet points for each module.
To write effective bullet points, say what they’ll learn + the benefit.
It helps to think of it as the opposite of pain points.
5) Proof
This depends on where you are.
If you have social proof, use it. If not, you can use yourself as a case study.
That’s what I did with my last cohort. It was the first time that I launched a product that was focused on Substack

If you don’t have proof, go generate some.
Either for yourself or for others.

Most people skim your sales page and immediately go to your FAQ to get the gist of it.

7) Send traffic
 
If you’re not making the monthly revenue you want, then you need more traffic.
The biggest mistake I see creators make is that they create a product and think that people will magically find their products.
Spoiler: They won’t.
You need to deliberately send traffic to your product.
More traffic = more sales.
So how do you increase traffic?
2 ways:
1) Increasing the frequency of your promotions
The cool thing about this is you don’t need to change anything.
You don’t need to change your offer, your content strategy, your funnel, or anything.
All you’re going to do is increase the frequency of your promotions.
You need to always remind people that you’re selling something.
Drill this in your head:Sales don’t come out of thin air
You need to be intentional with how you direct people.
You need to be clear on what products you have, what they need to do, how they can buy them, and when to buy them.
The chances of someone clicking on the link in your bio and buying from is astronomically small.
2) Creating more content
Creating more content gives you more in-between moments where you can promote your products.
This sounds obvious, but the more content you create, the more opportunities you’ll have for CTAs.
If you only post 7x a week, a weekly promotion post becomes 14% of your content.
People will get sick if you promote a product every time you post.
But if you’re posting 90x a month (which is 3 posts a day), and 4 of your posts are promotional posts, that’s only 4% of your monthly content as promotions.
More content = more opportunity to sell (without being annoying)
You can still provide value, create content you love, and build an audience, but you also get to make more sales because you simply have more CTAs in your content.
Promote your products like you’d promote a sponsorship.
